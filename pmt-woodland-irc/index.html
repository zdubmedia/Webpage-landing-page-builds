WHAT YOU'LL WORK ON

The step between beginner and advanced. Classroom time plus a full day on the range, with personalized feedback after every run. Tap any skill to see what it really means.

Smooth on AND smooth off. 01Throttle Control

The single most important control on your motorcycle. Beyond speed, the throttle affects traction, suspension, weight transfer, steering, stability and ground clearance. Most riders roll on smooth, then chop it off. You'll learn smooth on and smooth off, so your suspension stays settled into the turn instead of pogoing back and forth.

02Brake Control

A car pulls out and you need to stop in the shortest distance possible, without locking up and skidding. You'll practice max-effort stops (max pressure on the front brake, light pressure on the rear), rep after rep, with an instructor watching. By the end of the day, you'll know how to get on those brakes when it counts.

Look through the turn, not at the cones. 03Vision

Spotlight and floodlight: your central vision locked on where you're going, and your peripheral vision picking up everything around you. Head turns that lead you through the corner, and drills that build the habit like a muscle, so your eyes look through the turn instead of down at the front tire.

Turn in later. Exit with options. 04Line Selection

Turn point, apex and exit. Turning in early leads to an early apex that pushes you wide on the exit. You'll learn a later turn point and a later apex, which keeps you in your lane and gives you options on the way out.

Confident at 60, shaky at 5? Not anymore. 05Low-Speed Turns

Confident at 60, shaky at 5? Tight, slow turns using body position, and pushing down through the pegs to lean the bike quickly from side to side. Plus sharp turns from a stop, the move that makes riders on big bikes nervous.

06Evasive Maneuvers

Sometimes braking isn't the best move. When you have an escape route, you'll learn to swerve properly and efficiently around an obstacle and stay on two wheels.

The goal: no more running wide. 07Road-Speed Cornering

Where it all comes together: vision, line selection, a late apex, pre-positioning your body, countersteering, head turns and smooth throttle (set your speed before the corner, steady throttle through it). The goal: no more running wide.

Plus the mental side. Cornering strategies that cover what you're doing, when, and why, plus what to watch for so you spot trouble between cars and riders before it happens. Skill plus strategy is where riders level up.

WHAT TO BRING

All riders need full protective gear for the riding portions.

For check-in (everyone):

  • Photo ID
  • Your motorcycle license, if you're taking the Already Licensed option
  • Bringing your own bike? Its registration and proof of insurance

Gear checklist:

  • DOT helmet (full-face or modular)*
  • Eye protection
  • Full-finger gloves (leather or motorcycle gloves)*
  • Motorcycle jacket
  • Sturdy long pants with no holes
  • Over-the-ankle boots
  • Water and snacks

*Taking the licensing option? You can borrow a helmet and gloves from us.

Bringing your own bike?

It needs to be:

  • Street legal, registered and insured
  • On tires with at least 3mm of tread (no car, race or race-compound tires)
  • Free of fluid leaks, with a healthy battery and working brakes

QUICK ANSWERS

Is this class right for me?

If you already ride and want more control, or you're 21+ and need your license, yes. If you've never ridden a motorcycle, start with our Basic course.

What is the DL389?

It's the official certificate you earn by completing the licensing option: attend every classroom and range session, and pass both the knowledge test and the riding skills test. Take it to the DMV, and it waives the DMV riding skills test. You'll still complete the DMV's other license steps, like the written knowledge test. Your DL389 is good for 12 months from the day you finish, so take it to the DMV soon.

I'm under 21. Can I do the licensing option?

The licensing option is for riders 21 and older. If you're under 21 and need your license, our Basic course is the way to get it.

Is it all riding?

It's one 8-hour day with both classroom and on-bike instruction.

Does this count for military Level 2 training?

The IRC/1DPC is officially recognized for Level 2 training by the Army, Navy, Air Force, Marines and Coast Guard. Check with your base safety office to confirm what your command requires.

Can anyone sign up?

Riders with a license that's restricted because of an impaired-driving conviction can't take CMSP classes until all restrictions are lifted.

What if I need to change my date?

Call the office at (925) 373-4802 and they'll walk you through your options.
